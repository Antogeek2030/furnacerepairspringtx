Texas winters may be milder than other regions, but cold fronts still arrive — and a neglected furnace is the last thing you want when temperatures drop.

## 1. Replace or Clean Your Filter

A clogged filter restricts airflow, causes the furnace to work harder, and can lead to short cycling or overheating. Check monthly during heating season and replace as needed.

## 2. Schedule a Professional Tune-Up

An annual furnace maintenance visit catches small issues before they become no-heat emergencies. We clean critical components, test safety controls, and verify efficient operation.

## 3. Clear the Area Around Your Furnace

Keep at least 2–3 feet of clearance around the unit. Remove storage boxes, chemicals, and clutter that can create fire hazards or block airflow.

## 4. Test Your Thermostat

Set the thermostat to heat mode and raise the temperature a few degrees. Confirm the furnace starts and warm air reaches the vents. If nothing happens, call for service before the first hard freeze.

## 5. Check Vents and Returns

Make sure supply vents are open and unblocked by furniture or rugs. Restricted airflow reduces comfort and efficiency.
